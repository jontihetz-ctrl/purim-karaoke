The Manifesto

Fashion has always
policed.
This collection
dismantles all three.

Gender, geography, class — the industry built its empire on these borders. Who can wear this. Where it came from. What occasion it belongs to.

No Binary, No Borders answers none of those questions, because it refuses to ask them. These are garments that belong to whoever puts them on, wherever they are, whatever they are.

No him/her cuts. No country of origin. No season, no occasion. Just cloth on a body.

Visual Language

Four principles.
One collection.

■

Androgynous Silhouettes

Oversized, sculptural, draped. Built for shoulders, not gender. Every silhouette works on every body.

◼

Mixed Textile References

Kanga meets tartan. Kente alongside denim. Cultural conversation stitched into a single garment.

●

Earth + One Disruption

Neutral palette — warm sand, terracotta, deep brown. One shocking accent that refuses to be ignored.

○

Mixed Representation

Models of mixed gender presentation, age, and size. The collection dressed on the full range of bodies it was made for.

Four textiles.
One conversation.

Every fabric is credited. Every maker is a collaborator, not a source. This is conversation, not costume.

Kanga East Africa — Kenya, Tanzania

Cotton print cloth carrying Swahili proverbs. Every kanga tells a story — this collection makes that story part of the garment's dialogue.

Tartan Scotland — Highland tradition

A cloth of clan and belonging, hijacked by punk and reclaimed here. Pattern as identity, stripped of gatekeeping.

Kente Ghana — Ashanti & Ewe

Handwoven silk and cotton strips, each colour carrying meaning. Brought into conversation rather than display.

Denim Global — The great equaliser

The only truly classless cloth. The binding thread that ties every cultural reference into a single modern garment.
